# Scientific interpretation of E1

## What the experiment establishes

E1 is a ten-seed controlled intervention study under the calibrated generator regime. Every seed uses an independently generated Phase-1 population, ACS-initialized baseline households, and the same fixed 0.65 matcher threshold.

High noise reduced mean F1 from 0.9986 ± 0.0003 to 0.9774 ± 0.0010. The 0.0212 paired mean gap occurred in the expected direction for all ten seeds and was 28.63 times the pooled between-seed SD.

Low overlap reduced mean true links from 9,516.4 to 2,250.5. One-to-many increased mean Dataset B rows from 10,004.3 to 19,045.3 and candidate pairs from 66,050.5 to 126,600.1.

## Claim boundary

The supported E1 claim is that SOG produces reproducible, directionally correct changes in linkage difficulty, prevalence, and workload under controlled scenario interventions. E1 is not a generator-versus-generator superiority test and does not establish that any entity-resolution method improves because SOG is used.

Absolute F1 remains high outside the noise intervention, so E1 alone is a manipulation and stability result. The broader benchmark evidence comes from E2's matcher-specific degradation-profile spreads and household-formation hardness, and from E3's strictly monotone five-level noise gradient.

## Paper-ready conclusion

Across ten independently generated populations, high noise reduced fixed-threshold baseline F1 from 0.9986 ± 0.0003 to 0.9774 ± 0.0010. The 0.0212 mean gap was 28.63 times the pooled between-seed SD and appeared in all ten paired seeds. Low overlap reduced mean true links to 2,250.5, while one-to-many increased mean candidate workload to 126,600.1 pairs.
